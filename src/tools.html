The toolkit

The course teaches habits. These are the habits as working tools — bring your own material and use them on real work. Each one pairs with the module that explains it.

Set up for

Seven of these eight run entirely in your browser

Nothing you type here is uploaded — not to us, not to an AI company, not anywhere. You can check that yourself: turn off your wi-fi and they still work. That matters for a toolkit whose whole subject is what you should and should not send outside your building, and it means they need no setup, no account and no IT support. The registers save on your device, so they are still here next quarter — and they export as text when you need to share them.

Three of these implement guardrails from the Task Force's agency governance recommendations.

Before you paste

Redaction Check

Pairs with Module 3 — Data Privacy · Runs offline

Paste anything you are about to put into an AI tool. This finds the names, addresses, dates of birth, phone numbers, case identifiers and sensitive circumstances you missed. Use it on a case note, an email, a draft letter — anything with a person behind it.

It will not catch everything. In a county of thirty thousand, a detailed enough description identifies someone with no name attached at all. Treat a clean result as a good sign, not a clearance.

Before you ask

Prompt Builder

Pairs with Module 2 — How to Prompt · Runs offline

Most weak prompts are missing the same four things: who you are, what the tool needs to know, what shape the answer should take, and what it must not do. Fill these in and it assembles the whole briefing. If you have set up your agency profile, your details are already in.

Before you submit

AI-Tell Scanner

Pairs with Module 5 — Grant Writing · Runs offline

Paste a grant narrative and it marks the phrasing that reads as machine-written, then counts how many actual figures you used. The second number is the one that matters — specific writing does not sound generated, because the tool never had your data.

Before it goes to a funder

AI Output Check

Pairs with Module 4 — CSBG Reporting · Runs offline

Run this on every AI-assisted section before it is submitted. If any box cannot be checked, the section is not ready. It resets each time on purpose — this is a check you do per submission, not once.

From Module 4, by Meryem S. Golbasi.

Once a year

Reporting Assist Map

Pairs with Module 4 — CSBG Reporting · Saves on this device

One row per step in your reporting workflow, marking where AI helps and where a person must sign off. Fill it once and it is here next quarter. The first rows are examples — replace them with your agency's real steps, then copy it out for your team.

From Module 4, by Meryem S. Golbasi.

Guardrail 1

AI Tool Inventory

Implements guardrail 1 · Saves on this device

Which AI tools are in use at your agency, what for, who is responsible, and what client data each one touches. North Carolina requires this of state agencies; most CAAs are private nonprofits and fall outside that rule, which is exactly why keeping the record yourself is worth doing. It is also the first thing anyone will ask for if something goes wrong.

The two example rows are the situation most agencies are actually in. Replace them with what is true at yours — including the tools nobody formally approved, since those are the ones the inventory exists to surface.

Guardrail 7

AI Incident Log

Implements guardrail 7 · Saves on this device

When an AI tool gets something wrong, write down what happened. Your agency learns faster from a list than from memory, and pattern data across agencies is the only way anyone catches a tool failing systematically rather than once. Counts and patterns, never client detail.

Keep it free of client information. “Invented a figure in a grant narrative” is the entry; the narrative itself is not.

Before you submit 4A

FNPI Validator In build

Pairs with Module 4 — CSBG Reporting

Upload your Module 4A export and it checks the file before you submit it. This one is being built rather than described, because we already know it catches real errors: a review of twenty-four North Carolina FNPI exports in August 2026 found two defects that had passed through the normal process.

What it will check

  • Whether each displayed percentage matches the formula its own column heading claims
  • Domains left blank against a nonzero served count
  • Indicators carrying a target with zero actual
  • Targets of zero where people were served
  • Whether a file named as a rollup actually aggregates its component files

Every one of those runs on already-aggregated Served / Target / Actual counts, so the tool cannot touch personal information even by accident. That is the reason it is first in the build queue rather than something more impressive.

Something missing?

These came out of what agencies told us in interviews. If there is a repetitive, aggregate-data job eating your week that is not here, say so with the Feedback button — that is exactly how we decide what gets built next.

About these tools

Built by the NCAF AI Task Force, a student project at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, for the Community Action network. They are working drafts, not certified compliance software. Nothing here replaces your own review, and no tool on this page should be the last check before something reaches a funder.
